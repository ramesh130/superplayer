## Context

The forces at play: the problem, the constraints, the alternatives that were live at the time.
State facts, not conclusions. A reader who disagrees with the decision should still agree with
this section.

## Decision

One paragraph, in the active voice, saying what the project will do. Any binding rule that follows
from the decision is stated here as a rule, not as advice.

## Consequences

What becomes easier, what becomes harder, and what maintenance burden this creates. Include the
costs honestly — an ADR that lists only benefits is a sales document, not a decision record.

## Alternatives considered

Each rejected option, with the reason it was rejected. This is the section that saves the decision
from being re-argued from scratch.

## References

Public specifications, papers, and documentation that inform the decision. As with the citation
rule `CONTRIBUTING.md` places on code, every non-obvious technical claim in an ADR should be
traceable to a public source.
